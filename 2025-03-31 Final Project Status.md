For those who do not remember / didn't read what my final project is on, it is a tool that can predict future mental health disorders early based on a survey. While my idea for the tool is not to be used as something to diagnose patients, it could be used as warning signs. Especially when you can consider that at the start of many health checkups (especially for teenagers) they have patients fill out a survey about suicidal thoughts, apetite issues, boredom etc to try and see if the patient is showing any signs of depression, anxiety, or other mental health disorders. Specifically, my tool analyzes the correlation between external pressures and mental health disorders, things like academic stress, financial stress, social stress etc vs depression, anxiety, isolation. However now I am starting to think about if I should change the title from external pressures to percieved pressures. This is because the survey simply asks the participant on a scale of 1-5 without specifying ranges, ie 5 hours a week is 3/5. This means a student is more likely to put a higher number (indicating a higher pressure) if they percieve the pressure as higher. Take for example financial stress, take 2 students who both pay for rent and food, the one recieves a stipend from their parents while the other works for their money. Suppose both students recieve the same amount of money, the student who recieves the stipend might feel less financial pressure than the student who has to work for their money. There are other examples like if a student has less time than another student then even if they have the same academic workload the one with less time would experience higher levels of stress than the student who has more time on their hands.

My initial reaction to this was in order to improve the survey then we recollect data using a standardized approach. For example 1/5 indicating less then 2 hours, 2/5 would be 2-6 hours etc. However I realized this would subtract from the purpose of the survey because students who percieve a higher stress would indicate a higher pressure regardless of the actual magnitidue of the pressure they are feeling.
